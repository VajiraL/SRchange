# Phase 3: Global Threshold Proximity and Regime Mapping

## Overview
We translated the cluster-level thresholds identified in Phase 2 into global spatial datasets. For each grid cell, we calculated the distance to its cluster-specific threshold for Aridity Index and Human Modification (gHM). We also classified the land surface into two adaptation regimes.

## Adaptation Regimes
- **Regime 0 (Adaptive):** Grid cells where the feature value is below the detected threshold. In this regime, $S_R$ is actively responding (typically increasing) to changes in the predictor, indicating active ecohydrological adaptation.
- **Regime 1 (Saturated/Post-Threshold):** Grid cells where the feature value has exceeded the threshold. Here, the sensitivity of $S_R$ either plateaus or declines, suggesting that the ecosystem's capacity to adjust its root zone storage has reached a limit.

## Key Spatial Patterns

### 1. Aridity Threshold Proximity
- Large areas of the **Sahel, Central Australia, and parts of the Western US** are identified as being in Regime 1 (Saturated) for Aridity Index. These regions have likely exhausted their $S_R$ expansion potential under current hydroclimatic stress.
- **Boreal forests** show a sharp transition near the 0.84 AI threshold, with northernmost areas still in the Adaptive regime.

### 2. Human Modification (gHM) Proximity
- The "pristine sensitivity" detected in Cluster 0 (Boreal/Arctic) means that almost any human presence in these regions immediately pushes them into Regime 1.
- In more developed regions (Europe, Eastern US, SE Asia), the $S_R$ response to further modification is already suppressed.

## Significance
This mapping provides a spatially explicit view of ecosystem vulnerability. Regions in Regime 1 are "locked-in" or have lost their adaptive buffer, making them potentially more susceptible to extreme climate events that exceed their fixed $S_R$ capacity.
